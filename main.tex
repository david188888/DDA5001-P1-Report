\textbf{Setup.} Fixed course configuration: GPT-2-medium preset (24 layers, 16 heads, $d=1024$, 302.4M parameters), 65-character vocabulary, context 256, batch 72, 5{,}000 updates (92.16M tokens, $\approx$92 passes over the 1.00M-token training split), 100 warm-up steps, cosine decay to $0.1\times$ peak LR, dropout 0.2, no weight decay, clipping 1.0, bf16, \texttt{torch.compile}. Only the optimizer, its peak LR and \texttt{out\_dir} change. Muon updates the 2-D hidden matrices; embeddings, tied head, norms and biases use the auxiliary AdamW at $3\times10^{-4}$. One RTX 4090 per run (peak memory 21.8\,GB). \emph{Second LR:} default AdamW and Muon validation losses rose after 9--18M tokens, so I cut their LRs to $1/3$; SGDM's training loss fell slowly and smoothly, so I doubled its LR.

\section{Learning-rate effects and crossover}
\textbf{Crossover: overfitting decides the endpoint.} With 302M parameters and $\approx$92 passes over 1.00M characters, every run reaches its minimum validation loss early and then rises while training loss keeps falling (to 0.06--0.09 for AdamW and Muon). Fast optimizers reach the minimum first but also memorize first, so \key{the ranking reverses}: at default LRs, Muon (1.54) $<$ AdamW (1.68) $<$ SGDM (2.13) at 9.2M tokens, but \key{SGDM is lowest from 27.6M tokens}; from 36.9M both SGDM runs are below all four others, ending at \key{1.67} vs.\ 3.55--3.98 (AdamW) and 4.10--4.24 (Muon). The samples agree: Muon's is copied verbatim from the training text, SGDM's shares no 40-character window with it.

\textbf{Learning-rate effects.} \emph{AdamW}: lowering the LR to $10^{-4}$ slows early progress (1.77 vs.\ 1.68 at 9.2M) but delays memorization; the curves cross between 18.4M and 27.6M tokens, \key{the endpoint improves by 0.43 nats} and the copied share halves, while the minimum is almost unchanged (1.558 vs.\ 1.551). \emph{SGDM}: doubling the LR helps early (lower validation loss up to 27.6M), the curves cross between 27.6M and 36.9M, and the endpoint is almost the same (+0.005). \emph{Muon}: \key{lowering the LR did not help}; the curves cross twice ($3\times10^{-3}$ lower at 18.4M, higher from 27.6M) and the endpoint is 0.14 nats worse. A plausible reason, not tested here, is that the auxiliary AdamW, unchanged at $3\times10^{-4}$, still fits the embeddings and tied head. \emph{Time}: AdamW and SGDM take 0.36\,s per update; \key{Muon takes 0.41--0.42\,s (+14--17\%)} on an idle GPU, likely from its orthogonalization step.

\section{Difficulties, solutions and contribution}
\textbf{(1) Copied text.} The AdamW and Muon samples read like real Shakespeare. \emph{Solution:} I measured their verbatim overlap with the training split (Table~\ref{tab:results}, App.~\ref{app:copy}). \textbf{(2) Unfair timing.} The first default-Muon run shared its GPU with another process (0.59\,s per update). \emph{Solution:} I re-ran it on an idle GPU (App.~\ref{app:time}).

\section{Muon re-run}\label{app:time}
The first default-Muon run shared its GPU with another process and took 0.594\,s per update, against 0.410\,s when re-run on an idle GPU of the same model (RTX 4090). The re-run used the same seed (42); its endpoint validation loss is 4.100 against 4.085 (minimum 1.542 against 1.538), a small gap likely due to non-deterministic bf16 kernels. All reported Muon $10^{-2}$ numbers come from the re-run.

\section{Measuring copied text}\label{app:copy}
Each sample has 400 characters. I took every 40-character window with stride 5 (73 windows) and counted the windows that appear verbatim in the training split (the first 1,003,854 characters of \texttt{input.txt}). A window of 40 characters is long enough that a random match is very unlikely. Both Muon samples are identical because \texttt{sample.py} uses a fixed seed and both models reproduce the same training passage (\emph{The Winter's Tale}, Act~IV). Default-AdamW sample excerpt, copied from \emph{Henry VI, Part 3}:
{\small\begin{verbatim}
O Phoebus, hadst thou never given consent
That Phaethon should cheque thy fiery steeds,
Thy burning car never had scorch'd the earth!
\end{verbatim}}
